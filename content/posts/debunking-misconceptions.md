Whenever people online discuss  _Hán Nôm_ writing, they often do so by misconstructing narratives, saying half-truths, assuming certain things or through political narratives.

Some of those myths may arise from actual misunderstandings. Others, from oversimplified history classes. Our goal is not to compliment nor badmouth _chữ Nôm_, but rather to simply myth-bust.

## 1. "Hán Nôm is just Chinese"

While the backbone of the _Chữ Nôm_ writing system is _Hán tự_, it would be incorrect to assume that they're in any way part of the Chinese language, as _Nôm_ characters were systematically developed over the span of centuries to be adapted for the Vietnamese language.

## 2. "Nobody can read Chu Nom anymore"

Despite the small number of _chữ Hán Nôm_ users in comparison with writers of _chữ Quốc ngữ_, the former still remains an important tradition in Vietnam, being studied and used by scholars, hobbyists, caligraphers and Buddhist monks.

## 3. "You need to learn Chinese before you learn Nom"

This is utterly misleading. If this were true, Japanese citizens would need to learn Classical Chinese in order to learn to read and write in their own native language, which clearly isn't the case.

## 4. "Quoc ngu instantly replaced Nôm"

A number of factors were responsible for the downfall of sinogram-based writing, included but not limited to: French colonization; ease of teaching new generations to read and write in the new writing system; low number of literacy in the previous writing system. Overall, for decades, Classical Chinese, Hán Nôm and Quốc ngữ coexisted.

## 5. "Han Nom is a language"

This myth arises from the fact that, for several centuries in Vietnam, particularly those when Vietnam was under Chinese rule, _Hán văn_ was the language of the elite and educated class in Medieval times. Han Nom is a writing system, not a language in and of itself.

## 6. "Chữ Nôm is unpatriotic, it has no relevance today"

I often hear this sentiment from the anti-government people who call it "Hán nô" ('slave of China'), or say that China would "re-institute Han characters in Vietnam should they ever invade". While this is purely the writer of this article's opinion, chu Nom might as well just be one of the most intrinsically Vietnamese things to have ever been created.

## 7. "It's impossible to encode Nôm characters in computers"

Blatantly false. Thousands of those characters have been already encoded in Unicode, and the only measure necessary to be able to display them is a device that supports UTF-8 and custom fonts.

## 8. "Sino-Nôm is harder than Chinese"

Learning Sino Nom might be easier for the average Vietnamese speaker than it is to learn to read and write in Mandarin, simply for the fact that one would be learning a different writing system to write the same language, rather than learning an entirely new language and script.

## 9. "The Demotic script is useless, there's no point in learning it"

Almost any work written prior to 1920 contains Demotic characters to some extent. Would it be correct, therefore, to say that reading anything prior to that time isn't a useful skill to learn?

## 10. "Sino Nom is impossible to learn, it got replaced by the Roman script because it was inferior"

No script is "superior" or "inferior". While there is a significant learning curve to learning Sino Nôm, the truth is that studying this writing means one can deepen one's knowledge of Vietnamese culture and language.

# Afterthoughts

Nuances are more important over simpler narratives. Demotic writing has a long history of being used in Vietnam, and not all texts from the period have been translated or transcribed. Readers of this magazine are free to read the original texts and learn from them. No matter if you choose to learn it or not, just know that if you do, you can better understand a significant part of Vietnam's past history.
